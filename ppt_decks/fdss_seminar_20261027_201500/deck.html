Problem Statement

The Core Problem: Why Retail Calculators Lie

The dangerous mathematical gap between static expectation and volatile capital markets

$$\mathbb{E}[g(X)] \neq g(\mathbb{E}[X]) \quad \implies \quad \text{Deterministic linear compounding hides downside tail risk}$$
Jensen's Inequality: In non-linear multi-period compounding, expected wealth does not equal the compound average.
Conventional Retail Projection Deterministic
  • Assumes invariant annual return: $A(t) = P(1+r)^t$.
  • Projects lifetime wealth as an unrealistic straight line.
  • Completely ignores volatility ($\sigma = 0$ implicit assumption).
  • Fails to inform citizens about downside portfolio ruin.
Stochastic Capital Market Reality Stochastic
  • Asset prices fluctuate under continuous Brownian motion.
  • Volatility exerts a structural negative drag on compounded returns.
  • The typical median trajectory strictly lags the arithmetic mean.
  • Over 50% of real investors underperform advertised averages.

Differential Equations

Theoretical Pillar 1: Time Value & Governing ODEs

Linear accumulation versus infinitesimal autonomous growth in continuous time

Simple Interest: Linear ODE Non-Autonomous
$$\frac{dA}{dt} = P \cdot r, \quad A(0) = P \implies A(t) = P(1 + rt)$$
  • Constant growth rate depending only on original principal $P$.
  • Violates subadditivity: $A(t_1 + t_2) < A(t_1)A(t_2)/P$.
  • Restricted to money markets with tenors under one year.
Continuous Compounding: Autonomous ODE Exponential
$$\frac{dA}{dt} = r \cdot A(t), \quad A(0) = P \implies A(t) = P e^{rt}$$
  • Instantaneous rate of growth proportional to current wealth.
  • Euler's infinitesimal limit: $\lim_{m \to \infty} P(1 + r/m)^{mt} = P e^{rt}$.
  • Semigroup property: continuously compounded log-returns add linearly.
$$\text{EAR} = \left(1 + \frac{r_{\text{nom}}}{m}\right)^m - 1 \quad \bullet \quad r_c = m \cdot \ln\left(1 + \frac{r_d}{m}\right)$$
Regulatory & Quantitative Parity: Eliminating nominal APR illusions and converting discrete yields into continuous drift rates.

Debt Analytics

Theoretical Pillar 2: Consumer Debt Analytics

Finite geometric series summation and the negative amortization boundary theorem

$$P = \sum_{k=1}^n E (1+R)^{-k} \quad \implies \quad E = P \cdot \frac{R(1+R)^n}{(1+R)^n - 1} \quad \left(R = \frac{r}{12}\right)$$
Equated Monthly Installment (EMI): Derived via closed-form inversion of a finite geometric progression of discounted cash flows.
Amortization Dynamics Geometric Progression
  • Monthly accrued interest: $I_k = B_{k-1} \cdot R$.
  • Principal repaid: $P_k = E - I_k = P_1(1+R)^{k-1}$.
  • Principal repayments form an escalating geometric progression.
  • Early prepayments collapse total interest exponentially.
Negative Amortization Boundary Theorem Critical Trap
$$\text{Debt Amortizes} \iff E > P \cdot R \iff E > P \cdot \frac{r}{12}$$
  • If $E \le P \cdot R$, principal reduction is zero or negative.
  • Debt balance diverges monotonically: $\lim_{n \to \infty} B_n = +\infty$.
  • Exposes predatory credit cards and deceptive teaser-rate loans.

Numerical Optimization

Theoretical Pillar 3: Numerical Yield Optimization

Why Galois theory mandates numerical analysis for multi-period financial yields

$$\text{NPV}(r) = \sum_{t=0}^T \frac{C_t}{(1+r)^t} = 0 \quad \iff \quad \sum_{t=0}^T C_t x^t = 0 \quad \left(x = \frac{1}{1+r}\right)$$
Internal Rate of Return (IRR): Finding the yield root of a high-degree polynomial equation.
Galois Theory & Abel–Ruffini Theorem Insolvability
  • Polynomials of degree $T \ge 5$ have no general algebraic solution in radicals.
  • Financial cash flows regularly span 5, 10, or 20 multi-period tenors.
  • Closed-form high-school algebra is mathematically impossible.
  • Evaluating multi-year investment yields fundamentally mandates numerical methods.
Dual-Engine Numerical Root Solver Quadratic Rate
$$r_{k+1} = r_k - \frac{\text{NPV}(r_k)}{f'(r_k)}, \quad f'(r) = -\sum_{t=1}^T \frac{t \cdot C_t}{(1+r)^{t+1}}$$
  • Newton–Raphson Method: Quadratic convergence rate $|e_{k+1}| \le M |e_k|^2$.
  • Analytical first derivative avoids finite-difference precision loss.
  • Bisection Fallback: Guarantees convergence if derivatives oscillate or approach zero.

Theoretical Pillar 4: Stochastic Asset Dynamics & Itô's Lemma

Continuous-time Brownian motion and the mathematical proof of volatility drag

$$dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t \quad \implies \quad d(\ln S_t) = \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)dt + \sigma dW_t$$
Itô's Lemma Proof: Non-zero quadratic variation $(dW_t)^2 = dt$ introduces structural volatility drag $-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$.
Itô Stochastic Calculus Brownian Motion
  • Brownian paths have infinite variation: $(dW_t)^2 = dt$.
  • Taylor expansion includes second derivative: $f''(S) = -1/S^2$.
  • Closed-form discrete solution across time step $\Delta t$: $$S_{t+\Delta t} = S_t \exp\left[\left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)\Delta t + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t} Z\right]$$
The Volatility Drag Divergence Mean vs Median
  • Arithmetic Expected Mean: $\mathbb{E}[S_t] = S_0 e^{\mu t}$ (skewed upward).
  • Typical Median Trajectory: $S_{\text{med}}(t) = S_0 e^{(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)t}$.
  • Annual Drag Penalty: $\Delta \mu = -\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$.
  • For $\mu=12\%, \sigma=20\%$, drag is $-2.00\%$ per year, lagging arithmetic mean by ₹6.01 Lakhs per Million over 10 years!

Theoretical Pillar 5: Monte Carlo Simulation & Risk Estimation

Vectorized stochastic ensembles, Central Limit Theorem bounds, and Value at Risk

$$\text{Standard Error} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{sample}}}{\sqrt{M}} = \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\right) \quad \bullet \quad \hat{\mathbb{P}}(S_T \ge \text{Goal}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \mathbf{1}_{\{S_{T,m} \ge \text{Goal}\}}$$
Central Limit Theorem: For $M = 10,000$ simulated paths, estimation error bounds narrow to within $\pm 1\%$ of sample variance.
Vectorized Ensemble Convergence NumPy Engine
  • Generates $M = 10,000+$ independent stochastic price paths in sub-second vectorized runtime.
  • Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN): sample mean converges almost surely to true expectation.
  • Yields full empirical probability density function of terminal wealth.
  • Replaces blind single-point estimates with actionable probability distributions.
Downside Tail Risk Metrics Capital Preservation
  • Goal Attainment Probability: Empirical probability of achieving retirement target corpus.
  • Value at Risk (VaR 95%): Maximum expected loss at 95% confidence level over horizon $T$.
  • Conditional VaR (Expected Shortfall): Quantifies average catastrophic loss in the worst 5% tail scenarios.
